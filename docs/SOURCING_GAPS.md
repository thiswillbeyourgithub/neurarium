# Sourcing gaps

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Contract: this file lists the current gaps only.** Per gap: what is missing, why, and which corpus or next step could close it. Every figure lives in the generated block below (`python tools/update_readme_stats.py` rewrites it from `meta.provenance_stats` and the emitted data, the freshness test fails when it is stale), so the prose carries no hand-typed count, no date, and no history: a gap that closes is deleted, not marked done. `tools/tests/test_sourcing_gaps_doc.py` enforces this. How a pass was built belongs in `tools/README.md`, `docs/PROVENANCE.md` or `docs/DRUGS.md`.

Every node is graded (see `docs/PROVENANCE.md`); a bare `llm` grade and NOSOURCE both count as missing, `uncertain` is backed but flagged, and an UNKNOWN (∅) node was searched in every required corpus and stated by none. Made with the help of Claude Code.

## Receptors

### `receptor_synaptic`

The pre- versus postsynaptic site. GtoPdb (#12) has no such field, so it closes from prose only (the receptor's EN Wikipedia article #9 and Stahl Essential #3), and a `both` needs one quote per site. What is left:

- **One site found, value says both:** nAChR α4β2, GABA-B, δ-opioid, α2C.
- **Source contradicts the value:** 5-HT5A (its article calls it a presynaptic autoreceptor; flagged `contradicted`). mGluR6 is only covered by a group-level sentence that does not name it.
- **No sentence names a site:** β2, β3, GABA-A-rho, glycine, α2D, 5-HT1E, 5-HT1F, 5-HT4. The modulatory stubs (a2a, sigma-1, mt1, mt2, h4) sit outside the tally.

Next step: a neuropharmacology textbook (Rang and Dale, Katzung) for the "no sentence" group; 5-HT5A is a data decision.

### `receptor_sign`

What is left is sigma-1 (a chaperone, nothing states a sign) and the conflicts where our `modulatory` meets a source stating a direction. A sourcing pass never rewrites data to match a source, so these wait on a data decision:

- **a2a** (Gs coupling in its article), **mt1** (Stahl Essential's MT1-mediated inhibition), **h4** and **mt2** (GtoPdb #12 lists Gi/Go as the only primary transducer): flagged `contradicted`, sign stays `llm`.
- **cb1** stays unflagged: GtoPdb also lists Gs as a secondary mechanism, and its sources describe a brake on excitatory and inhibitory release alike, which is what `modulatory` means here.

Resolve against a textbook, then re-run the classification worklist and applier.

### `receptor_locations`

Allen AHBA (#8) limits, not oversights: **off-atlas or unsampled bases** (olfactory bulb, pituitary, subthalamic nucleus for some receptors) and **regions sampled but not detected**. Only autoradiography or a PET tracer atlas would reach them, at a licence and parcellation cost out of proportion to the count.

### `receptor_effect`

The UNKNOWN rows were searched across `unknown.UNKNOWN_REQUIRED_SOURCES` and nothing states an effect; the search expires like freshness (checked by `check_data.py`) and must be redone.

## Non-receptor targets

### `target_locations`

All SERT and NET terminal regions. A microarray measures mRNA in cell bodies, so a transporter confirms at its source nucleus (raphe, locus coeruleus) and cannot confirm the terminals where the protein works. Only a protein-level source (immunohistochemistry, a PET transporter atlas) would; the caveat already ships in the density caption.

## Projections

### `projections` (route)

Claustrum to insula stays NOSOURCE: the books give topography only, the one primate abstract states a connection with no direction, and the directional ones are mouse. Next step: a primate claustrum tract-tracing review.

### `projection_transmitter` and `projection_sign`

The candidates were read, not missed; no book sentence or Europe PMC (#17) abstract types these pathways:

- **Transmitter:** glutamate for thalamus to cingulate, cingulate to hippocampus, olfactory bulb to amygdala, insula to cingulate, claustrum to insula and the temporal anterior commissure; GABA for amygdala to hypothalamus and septum to hypothalamus; serotonin for raphe to accumbens, substantia nigra and septum; noradrenaline for every locus coeruleus arrow (the abstracts name the nucleus, not the transmitter); dopamine for VTA to temporal.
- **Sign:** corticopontine, fornix to mammillary, cingulate to hippocampus, olfactory bulb to amygdala, both claustrum arrows, insula to cingulate, septum to hypothalamus.

Septum to hypothalamus has mouse papers that write the septum only as "LS", which the endpoint filter never offers (an "LS" alias would be too loose). Neither kind supports UNKNOWN today, so they stay `llm`. Next step: a systems-neuroscience text (Purves) for the Papez and cerebellar loops.

The `blanket_claim` and `set_claim` flags (a sweep "to the entire neocortex", one sentence shared by several origins) are backed but uncertain by design: no sentence names the single lobe.

### Innervation coverage (the `check_data.py` innervation check)

Regions expressing a transmitter's receptors that no pathway of that transmitter reaches, by cause:

- **Duplicate of an existing arrow:** pituitary dopamine (the tuberoinfundibular pathway is the existing hypothalamus to pituitary arrow).
- **Intrinsic sources the model does not draw:** striatal acetylcholine (interneurons), olfactory-bulb dopamine (periglomerular cells) and acetylcholine (diagonal band).
- **Contested:** dopamine to the cerebellum.
- **No citable primate sentence:** dopamine to the occipital lobe, septum, pons, midbrain and medulla; serotonin to the subthalamic nucleus, claustrum and VTA; noradrenaline to the accumbens and VTA; acetylcholine to the hypothalamus, mammillary bodies, midbrain and medulla. The book sentences that name some of them describe rat data.

## Drug bindings

### `drug_bindings`

Bindings with neither a quote nor a Ki. PDSP (#5) and GtoPdb (#11) are exhausted for them:

- **The GABA-A modulatory site** (glutethimide, phenazepam, nimetazepam, butobarbital, cyclobarbital, secobarbital): no radioligand panel assays it and GtoPdb curates no row; Europe PMC abstracts either state nothing or were refused by the judge.
- **Judge-rejected Stahl quotes:** carbamazepine at glutamate, maprotiline at M1-M5 (`apply_binding_sources.py` keeps them out).
- **Prose gaps in the drug's own article:** prochlorperazine at α1A/B/D (its Ki row is a coarse α1 value), tropatepine and alimemazine at H1, trimipramine at Nav (Stahl says only "ion channels").

Next step: primary literature, else they honestly stay NOSOURCE. A known conflict stays unresolved and printed by the applier: Stahl explains sulpiride's sedation by H1 blockade while its EN article denies histamine affinity.

### `drug_binding_action`

- **`llm` directions:** a stated action with no quote behind it (the binding itself is backed by its Ki). The by-index direction passes (`fetch_binding_directions.py`, the Ki-table Action column, GtoPdb #11) are the route.
- **NOSOURCE:** active-metabolite `affinity_only` bindings the UNKNOWN search has not covered yet; run it over `metabolites.jsonl` too.
- **UNKNOWN:** searched in every corpus `UNKNOWN_REQUIRED_SOURCES` lists, none states the direction for that exact ligand and target (an abstract uses the drug as a tool compound, or names the receptor family, not the subtype). The search log expires and must be re-run.

### Measured Ki

The Ki-less drugs are the targets no binding database reaches: benzodiazepines and Z-drugs at the GABA-A modulatory site, neurosteroids, broad-mechanism anticonvulsants, enzymes and gases. The cited-paper route (`fetch_europepmc.py --ki-prose`) yields little: the values sit in tables of old papers with no open-access copy, and the corpus rule is open access only.

### `uncertain` nodes

Backed, but flagged by derived bullets (see `docs/PROVENANCE.md`); the bullets say why, node by node. Two limits stay open on bindings: the claims that do attribute the action to the drug (by name or pronoun) are deliberately not flagged, and telling a genuinely *wrong* claim from a weakly stated one needs independent evidence the drug lacks the property, which today only exists as a Ki on a coarse target we do not model.

## Drug facts

### `drug_categories`

- **Article stored but the category pass not re-run over it:** furosemide, bumetanide, cannabidiol, cannabinol, nabilone, rimonabant. Re-run `fetch_category_worklist.py` and the applier.
- **The lead does not state our class:** cyclobarbital, serdexmethylphenidate, tasimelteon, rubidium chloride, tiapride, xanomeline.
- **Label mismatch:** `naltrexonebupropion` carries `substance_use` + `appetite_suppressant`, but its article only calls it an obesity drug. Closing it is a data decision (relabel or drop `substance_use`).

### `drug_brands`

Some brand entries name no product (the placeholder `Other` in `drugs_data.jsonl`), and a few French/EU names have no stored page stating them. Fix the placeholders in `drugs_data.jsonl`; refetch the FR article or BDPM (#16) for the rest.

### `drug_half_life` and missing half-lives

The NOSOURCE values are kept on purpose: the combination products (whose half-life is per constituent), deutetrabenazine, and `methylfolate_l` (its only sentence is about the racemate). Closing them needs a per-constituent model. Drugs with no half-life at all either have no pharmacokinetics section in any stored corpus, or have none to state (a gas titrated to effect, ethanol's zero-order elimination, a combination product).

### Time-to-peak (`drug_tmax`)

Mostly jurisdictional: a drug never marketed in the US has no DailyMed (#14) label, BDPM (#16) covers only French single-active products, and EMA SmPCs are PDF. A label also states a peak conditionally (one strength, population, route or state), and none of those generalises to the molecule. The Wikipedia drugbox has no Tmax row, and its `Onset of action` row is clinical onset, not peak.

### First approval (`drug_first_approval`)

Two causes: the stored EN and FR articles carry no dated approval sentence (older benzodiazepines and barbiturates, phenothiazines, recreational and anaesthetic substances), or every candidate was refused (a patent or synthesis date, a later approval whose page names an earlier market, an ambiguous year, a substance never approved). Next step: a regulator's own record (BDPM's AMM date for France), which dates one country's approval, not the first anywhere.

### `drug_enzyme_strength`

The tier is only implied, never stated:

- **As a percentage:** zolpidem's minor routes, clonidine CYP1A2. A number a reader must bucket is not a stated tier.
- **By contrast:** lofexidine and pitolisant CYP2D6, nicotine CYP2B6, zonisamide CYP3A5.
- **Weak wording:** xanomeline CYP2D6 ("significantly contributes").

FDA's interaction table lists *sensitive* substrates (an AUC change under a strong inhibitor), an exposure class that does not map onto `major`. Next step: a DailyMed (#14) input to `fetch_cyp_worklist.py`, since labels often state the fraction metabolized in words.

### Metabolism coverage

- **Non-CYP routes outside `ENZYMES`:** AKR, SULT, DBH, and MAO, which is deliberately left out because a sentence naming it is usually about the drug acting on the enzyme, not being cleared by it.
- **Pronoun subjects:** a few genuine claims say "It" (reboxetine, selegiline and clomethiazole at CYP2B6); the subject rule refuses to guess, so hand-curating them is the cheaper route.
- **AGNP (#19) substrate-table rows not added:** UGT1A2 (a human pseudogene) and UGT3B7 (no such isoform), listed in `tdm.NOT_ADDED`. Where that table and our rows differ, it is one side silent, never a denial.
- **Metabolite `formed_by`:** hand-curated in `quotes/metabolism.py`; the rest stay NOSOURCE because the near misses are all wrong the same way (an enzyme that clears the metabolite, a sentence with no parent, a different metabolite of the same drug). Primidone's article says its forming P450s are unknown.

### Enzyme variability

PharmFreq (#13) indexes only CPIC level A genes, so CYP3A4, CYP1A2, CYP2A6, CYP2E1 and most UGTs have no profile by design, and no agreed diplotype-to-phenotype table exists for them (CYP1A2's variance is induction, CYP3A4's co-medication). An allele-frequency node (for example CYP3A4\*22 from gnomAD) is reachable but is a different claim from a phenotype frequency, so it needs its own node kind and display; whole-gene deletions (CYP2A6\*4, CYP2D6 copy-number variants) are invisible to that route anyway.

### CPIC rows not imported (#15)

`fetch_cpic.py` prints each exclusion on every run: genes that are not clearance speeds (HLA-A, HLA-B, SCN1A, the urea-cycle genes) and so have no frequency profile to pair with; CPIC's uncertainty phenotypes (`Likely` / `Possible Intermediate`, `Indeterminate`); levelled pairs with no guideline text yet; ondansetron (its rows are filed under tropisetron); and the paediatric population.

### Occupancy and brain entry

Turning the simulation's engagement index into an occupancy needs dose, bioavailability, volume of distribution, free fraction and the unbound brain-to-plasma ratio Kp,uu. Kp,uu has no open machine-readable source (the compiled sets are small, paywalled and mostly rodent), so the index is the honest ceiling. B3DB's total `logBB` (#22) tracks lipophilicity, not free drug at the receptor.
